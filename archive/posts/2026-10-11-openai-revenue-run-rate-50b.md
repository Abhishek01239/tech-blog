OpenAI has informed investors that its annualized revenue run rate stood at roughly $50 billion at the end of September 2026, according to reports from the Financial Times and others. This figure is approximately $20 billion below the nearly $70 billion level that had been widely reported in late September based on investor materials.

## What Happened

On or around October 8, 2026, the Financial Times reported, citing documents shared with investors, that OpenAI put its annualized revenue at approaching $50 billion as of the end of September. Reuters and other outlets confirmed similar details from sources familiar with the matter. Earlier reporting, including from Axios in late September, had indicated a run rate approaching $70 billion.

OpenAI did not respond to requests for comment from multiple outlets. The company is in early-stage talks to raise additional capital of at least $30 billion at a pre-money valuation near $1.4 trillion.

## Details

The discrepancy is primarily an accounting difference rather than a sudden drop in sales. Anthropic, OpenAI’s main rival, includes the full value of sales made through cloud partners such as Amazon Web Services and Google Cloud in its reported annualized revenue figures. OpenAI does not include the partners’ share in the same way; it books only its own portion for certain partner deals.

Investors had adjusted or compared the numbers on a more comparable basis, producing the higher figure. Bloomberg later reported that OpenAI expects its annualized revenue to reach or exceed $70 billion by the end of 2026 on its own accounting terms, driven largely by enterprise growth.

OpenAI has reported strong growth metrics alongside the figure: overall run-rate growth of about 77% in the third quarter and enterprise revenue growth of 107%. The company started 2026 with an annualized revenue of roughly $20 billion (versus about $6 billion in 2024). In the second quarter, Anthropic’s quarterly revenue exceeded OpenAI’s for the first time.

## Why It Matters

Annualized revenue run rates are closely watched as a proxy for demand in the AI sector, which is driving massive capital expenditure on chips, data centers, and power. The news contributed to pressure on related stocks: the Nasdaq closed down about 1.4% on the day of the reports, with Nvidia falling around 2.9%, Oracle about 5.5%, and other AI infrastructure names declining.

The clarification does not indicate lost customers or canceled contracts. It highlights the importance of consistent methodology when comparing frontier AI labs, especially as both OpenAI and Anthropic prepare for potential public listings. OpenAI has delayed its IPO to 2027, citing AI safety concerns, while Anthropic has discussed a possible listing as early as November 2026.

## Context and Impact

The AI infrastructure buildout continues at a rapid pace, with hyperscalers, chipmakers, and labs securing large financing packages. Accurate revenue signals help assess whether application-layer growth can support the capital intensity. OpenAI’s enterprise acceleration is viewed as a positive indicator of stickier, higher-value revenue, even as consumer growth remains significant.

The episode also underscores ongoing competitive dynamics. Anthropic has gained ground in enterprise, and both labs face scrutiny over safety, agent behavior, and the path to sustainable economics.

## What Next

Investors will watch whether OpenAI’s year-end target of at least $70 billion annualized revenue materializes on a consistent basis. Details of the new funding round, progress toward any public listing, and further disclosures around partner revenue accounting are likely to remain in focus. Related market reactions in AI infrastructure stocks will also be monitored.

## TechPulse Takeaway

The $20 billion gap is largely a measurement issue arising from different treatments of cloud-partner revenue, not evidence of a demand collapse. OpenAI’s reported growth rates remain strong, particularly in enterprise, and the company continues to target higher run rates by year-end. As frontier AI companies approach public markets, standardized and transparent revenue reporting will become increasingly important for investors evaluating the sector’s economics.
