How to read this table

Each row is one entry in the National Vulnerability Database, the US government's public catalogue of known software flaws. The tracker shows only entries published in the last 14 days with a CVSS severity of High (7.0–8.9) or Critical (9.0–10.0).

  • CVE: the flaw's permanent identifier. Click it to open the full NVD record, with affected versions, references, and vendor advisories.
  • Severity and score: the CVSS base score, which rates how bad a flaw could be in the worst case. It does not say whether attackers are actually exploiting it.
  • Published: when the NVD published the record, which is often days after the vendor disclosed the issue.
  • Summary: the first part of the official description. Read the full record before acting on it.

How to use it for patching

  1. Search for the vendors and products you run, for example wordpress, fortinet, or chrome.
  2. Open each matching CVE and confirm that your installed version is in the affected range.
  3. Check CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. A High flaw that is being exploited usually matters more than an unexploited Critical one.
  4. Apply the vendor's fix or workaround, starting with systems exposed to the internet.

Where the data comes from

The table first shows a snapshot that is rebuilt each week from the NVD's public API. Your browser then asks the NVD directly for the latest entries. No account is needed and nothing you search is stored or sent to this site. The NVD limits anonymous requests, so if a live refresh fails, the snapshot stays on screen.
