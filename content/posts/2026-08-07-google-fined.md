# Regulatory News: Google Fined $4.3 Billion
Google has been fined a record $4.3 billion by the European Union for abusing its Android dominance. The fine, imposed on July 18, 2018, is the largest ever imposed by the EU on a single company.

## What Happened
The EU found that Google had used its Android operating system to unfairly promote its own search engine and browser, limiting competition from rival firms. The EU's antitrust chief, Margrethe Vestager, stated that 'Google has used Android as a vehicle to cement the dominance of its search engine' and that 'these practices have denied rivals the chance to innovate and compete on the merits'.

## Why It Matters
The fine is significant not just because of its size, but also because it sets a precedent for how tech companies will be regulated in the future. The EU's decision is a clear signal that big tech companies will be held accountable for their actions, and that regulators will not hesitate to take action when they see anti-competitive behavior. As stated by Vestager, 'the commission's decision is a significant step in enforcing EU competition rules in the digital economy'.

## Industry Impact
The impact of the fine will be felt across the tech industry, as companies begin to re-evaluate their business practices. Google will be required to change its practices and give rival search engines and browsers equal treatment on Android devices. This could lead to increased competition and innovation in the search and browser markets, which could ultimately benefit consumers.

## Quotes
As stated by Google's CEO, Sundar Pichai, 'we are disappointed by the decision and will appeal'. However, Vestager remains firm, stating that 'the commission's decision is a clear message that the rules apply to everyone, including the biggest players'.

## What's Next
The fine is just the latest in a series of regulatory challenges facing big tech companies. As the EU continues to crack down on anti-competitive behavior, we can expect to see more fines and penalties imposed on companies that fail to comply with the rules. The impact of this decision will be felt for years to come, as the tech industry continues to evolve and grow.
