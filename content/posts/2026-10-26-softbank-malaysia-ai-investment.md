SoftBank Group was reported to be planning up to $50 billion in Malaysian technology investments, including artificial intelligence and data centers, after Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim said Masayoshi Son had identified potential project locations. The figure was Anwar’s account, reported by Bloomberg and relayed by CNBC; it was not announced by SoftBank as committed capital. SoftBank had not responded to Bloomberg or CNBC by publication, and the company’s separate October framework agreement expressly did not require the parties to proceed with a project. [1] [2] [3]

## What Anwar said

Bloomberg reported on October 25 that Anwar described a video conference with Son earlier in the week, during which Son had selected regions for technology projects, including data centers. CNBC’s October 26 report specified locations in Kuching, Sarawak, and peninsular Malaysia, and said Anwar described the prospective investment as spanning AI, new technologies and data centers. Neither report presented signed investment terms, a timetable for deployment or a SoftBank confirmation of the $50 billion figure. [1] [2]

The distinction matters: the reported amount is a proposed plan attributed to the Malaysian prime minister, not evidence that $50 billion has been transferred, contractually committed or allocated to particular facilities.

## A separate, narrower framework

SoftBank’s own October 8 announcement documented a framework agreement with Grab and Sarawak state energy company PETROS to explore an AI and digital-infrastructure platform in Sarawak. It described work on a phased-development roadmap and opportunities in AI compute, services, advanced technologies and robotics—not a $50 billion commitment. [3]

The release said the framework did not obligate the companies to enter definitive agreements or implement the contemplated project. It also listed outstanding dependencies, including due diligence, approvals, financing, power and customer demand. That primary-source caveat makes it especially important not to treat the framework as confirmation of Anwar’s much larger reported plan. [3]

## Malaysia’s ambitions—and the limits of the claim

Malaysia’s Ministry of Digital said in July that the government had launched AI Malaysia and a 2026–2030 national AI action plan, with data and computing infrastructure among its enablers. That provides policy context for the reported interest, but does not verify SoftBank’s proposed amount. [4]

The reported $50 billion remains an intention attributed to Anwar, not a signed investment commitment or a defined construction schedule.

## Why It Matters

The distinction between a prime minister’s account and a company commitment matters for anyone reading the $50 billion figure as money already destined for Malaysia. SoftBank’s own October framework with Grab and PETROS describes exploration without any obligation to proceed, and it lists due diligence, approvals, financing, power and customer demand as dependencies. Until SoftBank confirms terms, the reported plan remains an intention rather than infrastructure in development.
