Microsoft has opened a time-limited voluntary exit offer for eligible employees in participating teams in the United States, Canada, India and the United Kingdom. The program covers staff at level 70 and below, but it is not companywide: Microsoft says individual organizations decide whether to participate based on business priorities, customer commitments and workforce needs. Eligible employees were to receive details by email on Oct. 26, according to the company. [1]

The offer gives qualifying workers the option to leave with financial support and career-planning resources. Microsoft has not stated how many employees will accept it. CNBC, citing a person familiar with the matter, reported that about 60,000 employees could be eligible. That is an estimate of potential eligibility, not a forecast of departures or a company-announced reduction target. [2]

## What Microsoft has announced

Chief People Officer Amy Coleman described the offer as a way for employees already considering work outside Microsoft to make that transition with support, while giving participating teams another way to adapt. The company said the program builds on lessons from a voluntary retirement program earlier in 2026. Its public announcement did not disclose individual package amounts or a public decision deadline. [1]

Reuters also reported the roughly 60,000 potential-eligibility figure and quoted Microsoft’s stated country and job-level scope. It said some units—including marketing, commercial business and security—would be excluded, based on a source familiar with the matter. Those exclusions, like the overall headcount estimate, come from anonymous sourcing rather than Microsoft’s public announcement. [3]

## An option, not a confirmed headcount cut

The offer arrives as Microsoft invests heavily in AI computing infrastructure. Reuters reported that the company forecast $175 billion in capital expenditures for calendar 2026, while CNBC placed the offer amid the industry’s costly AI buildout and focus on capital efficiency. That is context, not a company-stated explanation: Microsoft’s public announcement did not say the program was a direct response to AI spending or attach a savings target to it. [2] [3]

Voluntary participation and organization-by-organization eligibility do not establish how many people will depart, or whether departures will reduce staffing overall. Microsoft’s earlier voluntary retirement program is precedent, not evidence of the outcome this time. The company has not published a final participation total or results for the new offer.
