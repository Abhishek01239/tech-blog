Grok Imagine posted a 7-minute, 23-second opening episode of an AI-generated retelling of Homer’s *The Odyssey* to X on Oct. 23, presenting it as Episode 1 of a series. The post credited the work to Grok Imagine and described a creative collaboration with Wonder Studios and director Dev Walker. [1]

The release was a short episode—not the full-length movie previously discussed by Elon Musk. TechCrunch and Variety reported on the post on Oct. 25, independently placing it in the context of Musk’s earlier stated ambition to produce a feature-length adaptation. [2] [3]

## What Grok Imagine posted

The official account invited viewers to watch the episode and offered a possible future breakdown of how it was made. Its post named Wonder Studios and Walker, but did not provide production details or substantiate claims about the process beyond saying it was made with Grok Imagine. The attached video’s runtime was 7:23, matching the outlets’ description of it as about seven minutes. [1] [2]

That makes the post evidence that an episode was published and how its account presented the work. It does not, by itself, establish the adaptation’s historical accuracy, the extent of human input, or any sole-authorship claim. The release should not be confused with a completed feature.

## From a stated film plan to an episode

In July, Musk had said Grok Imagine would make a full-length *Odyssey* movie by the end of 2026 and described the proposed film as historically accurate. Variety reported that promise and noted the Oct. 23 episode as a partial follow-through, not delivery of the feature itself. No full-length film announcement accompanied this episode post. [1] [3]

The distinction matters: the current news is the public release of a short installment, while the earlier full-film statement was a future target. The available sources do not verify that target’s completion or accuracy.

## Reporting versus reaction

TechCrunch’s report also included the writer’s critical assessment of the footage and its storytelling. Those judgments were explicitly the reviewer’s opinion, rather than independently measured qualities or claims made by Grok. Variety likewise reported the release and its relationship to Musk’s earlier plan; neither outlet’s coverage turns the account’s description into proof of historical fidelity. [2] [3]

For now, Grok has published an opening installment; a full-length version remains a stated future ambition rather than an announced completed film.

## Why It Matters

The episode is evidence that Grok Imagine published a seven-minute installment, not that the full-length, historically accurate film Musk described in July exists. The account named Wonder Studios and Dev Walker but supplied no production details, and the outlets’ assessments of the footage were reviewers’ opinions. Readers should treat the release as a short proof of concept whose accuracy and human input remain unverified.
