# DOJ, FTC Target Microsoft, OpenAI, Nvidia in AI Antitrust Probe

**Lead** – On June 12, 2024 the U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) and the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) announced a joint deal to investigate the AI‑centric activities of Microsoft, OpenAI, and Nvidia. The move marks the latest chapter in a growing slate of regulatory actions aimed at curbing the market power of the world’s biggest tech firms.

---

## What Happened

The DOJ‑FTC agreement, first reported by Reuters, outlines a coordinated approach to examine whether the three companies are leveraging their dominant positions to stifle competition in the artificial‑intelligence ecosystem. Investigators will focus on licensing terms, data‑sharing practices, and the bundling of AI services with cloud infrastructure. The agencies have not yet filed formal complaints, but the announcement signals that a formal antitrust case could be on the horizon.

> "Regulatory scrutiny is gathering steam amid concerns over concentration in the industries that make up AI," a senior FTC official said in a briefing.

The decision follows a similar 2019 pact that split enforcement responsibilities for Meta, Amazon, Apple, and Google. That earlier arrangement led to high‑profile lawsuits, including the FTC’s case against Facebook and the DOJ’s suit against Google.

## Why It Matters

Microsoft, OpenAI, and Nvidia sit at the nexus of the AI boom. Microsoft’s Azure cloud powers OpenAI’s flagship models, while Nvidia’s GPUs are the hardware backbone for most large‑scale training workloads. Together, they control critical layers of the AI stack—hardware, software, and cloud services—making it difficult for smaller startups to compete.

Industry analysts warn that unchecked dominance could slow innovation, raise entry barriers, and lock customers into proprietary ecosystems. For startups, the investigation could open doors to more equitable licensing terms and greater access to cutting‑edge models.

## Industry Impact

### Cloud and Platform Competition

If regulators deem Microsoft’s bundling of OpenAI’s models with Azure to be anti‑competitive, the company may be forced to unbundle services, allowing rivals like Google Cloud and Amazon Web Services to offer OpenAI models on a more level playing field.

### Hardware Supply Chains

Nvidia’s near‑monopoly on AI‑grade GPUs has already prompted concerns about supply‑chain resilience. An antitrust finding could compel Nvidia to license its GPU architectures more broadly, potentially spurring the emergence of alternative chipmakers.

### Startup Ecosystem

Startups that rely on API access to large language models could benefit from reduced pricing pressure and clearer data‑usage rules. The FTC’s 2024 executive order on data collection already pushes big tech to be more transparent, and this probe could extend those principles to AI data pipelines.

### Global Ripple Effects

U.S. actions often reverberate abroad. The European Union is concurrently tightening its AI Act, and the UK’s Competition and Markets Authority has hinted at similar reviews. A coordinated U.S. stance may encourage allied regulators to adopt comparable frameworks, shaping a more unified global approach to AI governance.

---

## What's Next

Both agencies have set a 12‑month timetable to complete their fact‑finding phase. Expect subpoenas, depositions, and a flood of internal documents from the three firms. While no lawsuit is guaranteed, the precedent set by earlier antitrust cases suggests that a settlement—potentially involving divestitures or licensing reforms—could be on the table before 2025.

For the broader tech community, the probe underscores a pivotal moment: innovation must now coexist with heightened regulatory oversight. Companies that proactively address competition concerns may find themselves better positioned in an increasingly scrutinized AI landscape.
