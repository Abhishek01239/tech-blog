# CERN and Mistral Partner on Flare, the First Large Physics Model for the LHC

CERN, the European Laboratory for Particle Physics, and French AI company Mistral AI have signed a statement of intent to develop foundation models for scientific discovery. Their first project is Flare, described by CERN as the first Large Physics Model for collider physics. The announcement came on October 15, 2026.

## What Flare Aims to Do

Flare will be pretrained on billions of simulated proton-proton collision events from the Large Hadron Collider (LHC). Unlike specialized algorithms used today for individual tasks, the partners intend it to serve as a general-purpose foundation model capable of multiple jobs:

- Reconstructing particle collisions from detector signals
- Identifying the particles produced in each event
- Flagging unexpected signals that could point to new physics
- Evaluating designs for future detectors

CERN has used machine learning for about a decade to turn raw experimental data into analyzable events, but currently relies on separate tools for each stage. Flare is meant to replace or unify some of those pipelines.

## Why CERN Needs It Now

CERN already holds roughly one exabyte (about one million terabytes) of experimental data from the LHC. The High-Luminosity LHC upgrade, expected to begin operations around 2030, is projected to increase collision rates by a factor of ten. Existing analysis methods were not designed for that volume. A single pretrained model that can handle reconstruction, identification, and anomaly detection is seen as a way to keep pace.

CERN Chief Information Officer Enrica Porcari said high-energy physics offers a unique environment in which artificial intelligence can be developed and tested with exceptional rigour. The partnership is intended to advance AI capabilities that can serve the broader physics community and, later, other sciences.

## How the Partnership Works

CERN contributes its physics expertise, scientific datasets, and large-scale computing resources. Mistral contributes experience training large models. The collaboration is framed as a long-term strategic partnership rather than a one-off project. No formal budget, delivery timeline, or contract details have been disclosed. Validation on real detector data (the sim-to-real gap) remains a key open question before any model can fully replace existing specialized pipelines.

## Broader Context

The move comes as Mistral continues to position itself as Europe’s leading independent AI lab. For CERN, it is a step toward applying the foundation-model approach that transformed language and vision to the structured, high-precision domain of collider data. If successful, techniques developed for Flare could influence how other large scientific facilities handle growing data volumes.

## Why It Matters

Particle physics has long been a proving ground for computing and data-intensive methods. Bringing a foundation-model architecture to LHC analysis could change how physicists search for rare signals and design the next generation of detectors. It also strengthens Europe’s capacity to develop domain-specific AI at the frontier of both science and machine learning. The real test will be whether Flare delivers measurable gains in reconstruction accuracy, anomaly detection, and scalability once the High-Luminosity LHC era begins.
