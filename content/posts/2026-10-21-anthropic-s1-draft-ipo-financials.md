# Anthropic's Draft S-1: $16.3 Billion in Revenue, an $80.7 Billion Loss, and the Biggest IPO of the Year

The numbers inside Anthropic's draft S-1 registration statement read like two different companies. One is a business growing at a pace almost unmatched in corporate history. The other is an enterprise carrying some of the largest losses and financial commitments ever disclosed by a private firm about to go public. On October 20, the Wall Street Journal reviewed the draft prospectus for the San Francisco-based AI lab, and the filing offers the most detailed picture yet of the economics behind the Claude developer — just weeks before an initial public offering that could rank among the largest ever.

## Revenue up nearly 15x

The headline growth figure is staggering. Anthropic generated $16.3 billion in revenue during the first half of 2026, up from roughly $1.1 billion in the same period a year earlier — a nearly fifteen-fold increase. Momentum accelerated through the year: third-quarter revenue reportedly reached $16.8 billion, up about 45% from the prior quarter, and the company says it achieved adjusted profitability and positive free cash flow in that quarter, the second consecutive quarter of adjusted profit.

Behind the top line sits a striking efficiency metric for a company of this profile: the filing indicates Anthropic generated about $1.70 in revenue for every dollar spent on compute. That ratio has become the industry's favorite yardstick for separating real AI businesses from hype, and Anthropic's figure positions it favorably against rivals still burning several dollars of compute for each dollar of revenue.

## An $80.7 billion loss — mostly on paper

Then comes the other half of the ledger. Anthropic reported a net loss of $80.7 billion for the first half of 2026, compared with a $13 billion loss a year earlier. But the number is less alarming than it first appears: roughly $72 billion of it stemmed from non-cash accounting adjustments tied to convertible notes and other financing instruments. As Anthropic's valuation climbed — the company reached a private valuation of $965 billion in May — those paper charges ballooned. The company also recorded a GAAP net loss of about $42 billion for all of 2025 under the same dynamic.

Still, the cash costs are real and enormous. Anthropic spent nearly $9 billion on compute and infrastructure in the first six months of 2026 — already more than its $7.3 billion for the entirety of 2025.

## A $750 billion compute bill

The most consequential disclosures concern the future. Anthropic has locked in roughly $450 billion in cloud compute agreements over the next seven to ten years, including $111 billion with Google Cloud, $110 billion with Amazon Web Services, and $31 billion with Microsoft, according to the filing. Total committed compute and infrastructure spending now stands at more than $600 billion over roughly a decade — up from a previously disclosed $518 billion — and around 80% of those commitments are non-cancellable. If additional agreements still under negotiation are signed, total future infrastructure spending could reach $750 billion.

The prospectus also acknowledges that Anthropic has sought more capacity than the major hyperscalers were willing to supply, prompting the company to pursue third-party data center developments to work around compute bottlenecks. The filing reportedly includes a candid "catastrophic incident" warning among its risk factors — an unusually direct acknowledgment of the potential dangers of frontier AI development.

## Concentration risk and the road to $2 trillion

Investors will also have to weigh significant customer concentration. Roughly 20% to 25% of first-half revenue came from just two unnamed customers, and those relationships are not bound by long-term contracts. For a company targeting a valuation of up to $2 trillion at its November IPO — led by Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley — that dependency is the kind of risk factor underwriters tend to highlight in bold.

The public benefit angle adds another layer. Anthropic recently pledged to dedicate roughly 20% of its shares to a "public benefit" fund aimed at addressing the economic effects of AI, a commitment announced alongside its earlier $200 million pledge to study AI's labor-market impacts. Critics have been split: some see genuine structural innovation in AI governance, while skeptics, including NYU professor emeritus Gary Marcus, argue the company is "playing games" by currying public favor while resisting fuller disclosure.

## The AI economy's stress test

Anthropic's draft S-1 arrives at a moment of intense scrutiny for the AI sector, with chip stocks recently rattled by bubble fears and regulators probing AI market power. Whether Wall Street embraces a company with 15x revenue growth, positive free cash flow, a paper loss approaching the GDP of a mid-sized country, and three-quarters of a trillion dollars in compute commitments will say a great deal about how much faith investors still place in the AI buildout. The offering, expected as soon as November, may be the last major AI IPO of 2026 — and it is shaping up to be the defining test of the entire industry's financial model.
