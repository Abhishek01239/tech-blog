# Google DeepMind Unveils Genie 4: World Model With Native Audio and Real-Time Steering

Google DeepMind has released Genie 4, its latest world model, marking a significant step forward in real-time interactive simulation. Announced on October 27, 2026, the model generates photorealistic environments that respond to user input while producing native audio—including ambient sounds, music, and interactive dialogue—all in one continuous generation pass.

Unlike earlier Genie versions limited to shorter clips and separate audio tracks, Genie 4 supports simulations lasting up to two minutes, doubles the coherence window of its predecessor, and integrates more tightly with the Gemini model family. Gemini Omni anchors visual realism, while Gemini 3.8 Flash acts as an in-the-loop agent that observes the scene, plans events, and maintains continuity as the world evolves.

## Real-time control and native sound

Users navigate Genie 4 worlds with standard keyboard controls and steer them mid-simulation using plain language. Prompts can change the weather, reposition the camera, introduce new characters or objects, or trigger events, and the model adapts contextually. Characters move more naturally, respond to environmental changes, and can engage in dialogue that updates in real time.

The audio breakthrough is especially notable. Genie 4 is DeepMind’s first real-time world model to generate sound natively: wind in trees, waves on a lake, saxophone music, or spoken responses appear synchronized with the visuals rather than layered afterward. Official demos show a saxophone player on a floating platform in a misty lake, a flowing pink silk scarf through a wildflower meadow, and other cinematic scenes, all with matching soundscapes produced by the model itself.

DeepMind researchers Raia Hadsell, Diego Rivas, and Hang Qi described the advance in the official blog post: Genie 4 “unlocks unprecedented realism and improves real-time interactive simulation” and is “natively multimodal.”

## Rollout and practical uses

Genie 4 is currently available as a research preview to trusted testers. It will soon reach Google AI Ultra subscribers through Project Genie and early-access partners via the Gemini API and Google AI Studio. A waitlist is open for Project Genie access. All generated content carries SynthID watermarks.

The lab highlights applications in robotics training (safe, variable simulation environments), filmmaking pre-visualization, game prototyping, education, and creative tools. Early tester feedback has emphasized improved photorealism, dynamic events, and the value of real-time audio.

## Why it matters

World models are widely viewed as a critical path toward more capable AI agents and, ultimately, AGI. By combining longer coherent simulations, native multimodal output, and interactive steering, Genie 4 narrows the gap between generated video and usable interactive environments. Competitors such as Runway have also advanced world models, but DeepMind’s tight Gemini integration and native audio set a new bar for what research previews can demonstrate.

For developers and businesses, the phased rollout means early experimentation will be limited to high-tier subscribers and partners, with broader API access expected to follow. Pricing for the Gemini API tier has not yet been detailed. As more labs ship controllable world models, expect rapid progress in synthetic data generation for robotics and richer creative tools for media production.
