Tata Communications says it plans to invest $400 million to $450 million in about 10,000 kilometers of new long-haul fiber across India. Announced on Oct. 26, the proposed buildout is intended to connect data-center and AI hubs rather than add computing capacity itself. The company says it will develop the network in three phases; the spending and construction remain plans, not completed infrastructure. [1] [2]

## A phased terrestrial buildout

The first phase is set to connect Mumbai, Pune, Hyderabad and Chennai, which Tata identifies as a major AI and hyperscale growth corridor. Later expansion is intended to reach eastern and western hubs, including Vizag, Bhubaneswar, Jamnagar and GIFT City. Tata says the new fiber will link key corridors and data-center clusters with international cable landing stations and integrate with its global network backbone. [1] [3]

The headline figure refers to approximately 10,000 kilometers of planned new long-haul fiber, not routes that are already built or a guarantee that every listed location is part of the first phase. Tata’s release gives an investment range of $400 million to $450 million; Reuters described the announced amount as close to $450 million. [1] [2]

## Customers and commercial rationale

The company says the network is intended for hyperscalers, neocloud providers, AI platforms, digital-native businesses, global capability centers and enterprises. Its stated aim is to provide high-capacity, low-latency and resilient connections between facilities handling AI workloads. That is a connectivity proposition: the announcement does not describe a new data-center campus or an AI-chip deployment. [1]

Tata Communications CEO Ganesh Lakshminarayanan said the data-center-to-data-center connectivity market in India could exceed $1 billion over the medium term. That is the company’s market estimate, not a revenue forecast for this project. The release also says a growing share of its expansion is driven by anchor-customer commitments and contracted demand, without naming customers or quantifying those commitments. [1]

## Timing and execution remain open

Moneycontrol reported that investment is expected to be deployed over three to five years and quoted network-services executive Rupesh Chokshi saying customer discussions were advanced, but contracts for this proposed network had not been announced. Delivery timing by route and customer uptake therefore remain ahead. [4]

India’s AI infrastructure discussion often centers on compute, power and data-center capacity. Tata’s proposal instead targets the terrestrial links connecting those facilities. Whether the planned network meets demand will depend on execution and customer adoption; the announcement alone does not establish that the fiber is operational. [1] [4]

## Why It Matters

Tata’s plan targets the links between AI facilities rather than the compute inside them, a layer that receives less attention than chips and data-center capacity. The $400 million to $450 million and 10,000 kilometers are plans spread over phases, and contracts for the proposed network had not been announced. Execution and customer uptake, not the announcement, will determine whether the corridor becomes operational infrastructure.
